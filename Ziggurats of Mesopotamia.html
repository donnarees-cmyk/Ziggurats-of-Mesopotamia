Ziggurats: Mountains Made of Mud

Five thousand years ago, people in Mesopotamia built giant stepped towers called ziggurats. Each one rose high above its city like a man-made mountain. In this tutorial you will learn why they were built, which civilizations built them, and you will build one yourself in 3D.

Part 1

The Land Between the Rivers

Mesopotamia means "the land between the rivers." It sat between the Tigris and Euphrates Rivers, in what is now mostly Iraq. Floods left rich soil called silt, so farmers grew extra food, called a surplus. A surplus meant not everyone had to farm. Some people became priests, scribes, builders, and traders. Villages grew into the world's first cities.

Southern Mesopotamia had almost no stone and few trees. What it had was mud. So people shaped river clay into bricks, and built their greatest buildings out of them.

Ziggurat or Pyramid?

Ancient Egypt grew up along another river, the Nile. Egyptians built pyramids. They look a bit alike, but they had very different jobs. Turn on Show Egyptian pyramid in the 3D builder to see them side by side.
